# Opportunity Equity Engine — Initiative

## 1. Initiative statement

The Opportunity Equity Engine is a student-centered platform that helps high school students discover, understand, and prioritize educational, career, volunteer, research, competition, scholarship, and summer opportunities.

It uses structured information, deterministic eligibility checks, search, ranking, and carefully grounded AI assistance to make scattered opportunity information easier to use.

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this initiative is to reduce the information and confidence gap that prevents capable students from finding opportunities that could help them grow.

Many students do not lack ambition. They lack:

- A clear place to start.
- Time to search many websites and PDFs.
- Confidence that an opportunity fits their grade or location.
- A simple way to compare costs, deadlines, and requirements.
- Guidance about what action to take next.

The platform turns scattered information into a more understandable and actionable set of possibilities.

## 3. Ultimate goal

The ultimate goal is:

> To give every student a clearer and more equitable path from curiosity to opportunity.

The project should help students move through four stages:

```text
Discover → Understand → Prioritize → Act
```

The system is not intended to guarantee admission or decide a student's future. Its purpose is to make relevant possibilities easier to find and informed next steps easier to take.

## 4. Problem being resolved

Opportunity information is often fragmented across:

- Organization websites.
- School announcements.
- Scholarship pages.
- Competition portals.
- Research program pages.
- Volunteer listings.
- PDFs and application documents.
- Social media posts and newsletters.

This creates several problems:

1. **Discovery problem:** students never hear about many opportunities.
2. **Eligibility problem:** requirements are difficult to interpret or compare.
3. **Prioritization problem:** students cannot easily decide which opportunities deserve attention first.
4. **Deadline problem:** important dates are easy to miss.
5. **Access problem:** students with less guidance or fewer connections may be disadvantaged.
6. **Trust problem:** old, incomplete, or unclear information can lead students to waste time.

The Opportunity Equity Engine addresses these problems by organizing verified information, applying clear rules, ranking relevant options, and showing the evidence behind recommendations.

## 5. Target audience and end users

### Primary users

High school students, initially focused on students in Montreal and Quebec.

Typical users may be:

- Grade 9–12 students.
- Students exploring STEM, medicine, technology, arts, leadership, research, or community service.
- Students who do not yet know which opportunities exist.
- Students who need affordable, local, online, or language-accessible options.
- Students who want to explore without sharing unnecessary sensitive information.

### Secondary users

- School counselors.
- Teachers and mentors.
- Parents or guardians supporting opportunity exploration.
- Community organizations serving high school students.
- Administrators who verify and maintain opportunity information.

### Not the primary audience initially

- Universities managing their own admissions.
- Employers running professional recruiting systems.
- Users seeking automated application completion.
- Organizations that want unverified promotional placement.

## 6. Value proposition

### For students

> Find opportunities that may fit you, understand why, and know what to do next.

### For counselors and mentors

> Give students a structured starting point for opportunity conversations and planning.

### For opportunity providers

> Make accurate program information easier for eligible students to discover.

### For the student team

> Learn how to turn a real social problem into a useful, tested, and responsibly deployed AI product.

## 7. How the product creates value

```text
Collect permitted source information
              ↓
Extract structured opportunity fields
              ↓
Validate and review the information
              ↓
Match requirements against a student profile
              ↓
Rank relevant opportunities
              ↓
Explain the recommendation with evidence
              ↓
Help the student save and act on a next step
```

The product combines AI with traditional software:

- AI interprets unstructured language.
- Code enforces deterministic eligibility rules.
- Search and ranking organize results.
- Human review protects data quality.
- The student makes the final decision.

## 8. Initial product scope

The first release should include:

- A simple student profile.
- A curated opportunity database.
- Grade, location, interest, cost, language, and deadline fields.
- Search and structured filters.
- Basic deterministic eligibility checks.
- Transparent match scores.
- Source links and verification dates.
- Grounded explanations.
- Saved opportunities and deadline reminders.
- An administrative review workflow.

The initial focus should be one geography and a limited number of opportunity categories. Quality, relevance, and verifiability matter more than the number of records.

## 9. What the initiative will not do

The initiative will not:

- Guarantee acceptance into any program.
- Replace official organization eligibility decisions.
- Collect unnecessary sensitive personal information.
- Invent deadlines, requirements, costs, or program benefits.
- Become an AI essay-writing service.
- Submit applications automatically in the MVP.
- Promote paid opportunities without clear labeling.
- Pretend that a match score is an objective measure of a student's potential.

## 10. Guiding principles

### Equity before popularity

The platform should help students discover relevant options, not only the most famous programs.

### Clarity before complexity

Students should understand why a result appears and what action is possible next.

### Evidence before confidence

Every important recommendation should be connected to a source and a verification date.

### Code for rules, AI for language

Hard eligibility decisions should be deterministic and testable. AI should assist with interpretation, extraction, and explanation.

### Privacy by design

Collect the minimum information needed for matching and make deletion possible.

### Human review matters

AI-extracted information should be checked before it becomes student-facing data.

### Creativity is encouraged

Students should use vibe coding and AI tools to experiment with interfaces, categories, explanations, and new ways to help users. Technical tools should expand their ideas, not limit them.

## 11. Expected impact

### Short-term impact

- Students spend less time searching through disconnected sources.
- Students understand basic eligibility before applying.
- Students see opportunities that may otherwise remain hidden.
- Students can organize deadlines and next steps.
- Counselors have a clearer conversation tool.

### Long-term impact

- Broader awareness of educational and career pathways.
- Better access for students without extensive guidance or personal networks.
- More informed student decisions.
- A reusable model for responsible AI-supported opportunity discovery.

The project should measure impact rather than assume it. A pilot can ask whether users found a new opportunity, understood the recommendation, and felt more confident about what to do next.

## 12. Success measures

The initiative is progressing when:

- Students can complete the main flow without assistance.
- Users discover opportunities they did not already know about.
- Recommendations include understandable reasons and source links.
- False eligible recommendations are rare and investigated.
- Expired or uncertain information is clearly labeled.
- Students report greater clarity about their next step.
- Administrators can correct or retire inaccurate records.
- The student team can explain the system's strengths and limitations.

Useful pilot questions include:

```text
Did you find an opportunity you would not have found otherwise?
Did you understand why it was recommended?
Did you know what to do next?
Did any requirement, cost, or deadline appear unclear or incorrect?
What would make this tool more useful to you?
```

## 13. Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Interest in the initiative | How to involve them |
|---|---|---|
| Students | Find relevant and accessible opportunities | Interviews, usability testing, pilot feedback |
| Counselors and teachers | Help students plan and discover options | Expert review and pilot use |
| Parents or guardians | Support safe and useful exploration | Privacy and safety review |
| Opportunity organizations | Reach eligible students accurately | Source verification and link review |
| Student project team | Build, learn, and demonstrate impact | Ownership, documentation, and retrospectives |
| Adult mentor | Review technical, privacy, and scope risks | Periodic design and release review |

## 14. How the team should work

The project should be run as a student-led product initiative:

1. Start with a real user problem.
2. Define a small experiment.
3. Use vibe coding to build quickly.
4. Review and test AI-generated work.
5. Talk to real users.
6. Improve based on evidence.
7. Document decisions and limitations.

Students do not need to become experts in every programming language or cloud service. They do need to understand the product's data flow, verify important results, and make responsible decisions.

## 15. Future possibilities

After the MVP is trusted, the initiative could explore:

- More regions and languages.
- Better source freshness monitoring.
- Counselor dashboards.
- Opportunity comparison tools.
- Personalized deadline planning.
- Accessibility improvements.
- Outcome measurement and recommendation evaluation.
- Partnerships with schools and community organizations.

These are future possibilities, not commitments for the first release.

## 16. Final positioning

The Opportunity Equity Engine is more than a directory and more than a chatbot. It is a responsible opportunity-discovery system that combines:

```text
Verified information
+ Structured data
+ Deterministic eligibility
+ AI-assisted understanding
+ Transparent ranking
+ Human review
+ Student creativity
```

Its ultimate purpose is simple:

> Help more students see meaningful possibilities, understand their options, and take a confident next step.
