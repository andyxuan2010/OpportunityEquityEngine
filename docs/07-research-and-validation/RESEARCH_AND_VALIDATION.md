# Research and Validation: Learn Before You Scale

Students should not build from guesses. Research turns an interesting idea into a useful product and gives the team evidence for each important decision.

## What to validate first

Start with five questions:

1. Can students currently find the opportunities they care about?
2. Which filters and explanations help them decide?
3. What information makes them distrust a recommendation?
4. Can a student complete the main task without adult coaching?
5. Does the product improve clarity, or merely add another place to search?

## A safe student interview

- Ask for permission before taking notes or recording. Recording is optional.
- Explain the purpose, the time required, and that participation is voluntary.
- Ask about recent behavior, not only opinions: “Show me how you look for programs.”
- Do not ask for grades, immigration status, financial details, or other sensitive information unless it is essential and approved.
- Remove names and identifying details from notes. Store notes only where the team agreed.
- Never promise admission, funding, or a particular product outcome.

## A practical research note

Record the date, participant type (not their name), question, observed behavior, quote or evidence, interpretation, and the decision it supports. Separate what you observed from what you inferred.

## Verify opportunity data

For every opportunity, keep the official source URL, page title, verification date, location, eligibility, cost, deadline, language, and a short evidence note. Prefer the official program page. If a detail is unclear, show “needs verification” instead of guessing.

## Usability test script

Give a student this task: “Find one opportunity that fits your interests, confirm why it fits, save it, and identify the next action.” Do not teach the interface during the first attempt. Observe where they hesitate, what they misunderstand, and whether they can explain the recommendation in their own words.

Afterward ask:

- What did you expect to happen?
- What was confusing or missing?
- Which information helped you trust the result?
- What would you do next outside the product?

## Useful measures

Track task completion, time to first relevant result, incorrect interpretations, source verification success, and confidence before versus after the task. A smaller number of trustworthy recommendations is better than a larger number of noisy results.

## Research decision log

```text
Question:
Evidence collected:
What we observed:
What we believe (label as an assumption):
Decision:
What would change our mind:
Owner and review date:
```

Do not treat a small student sample as universal truth. State who participated, what was tested, and what remains unknown.
