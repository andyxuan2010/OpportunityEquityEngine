# Showcase and Reflection: Prove What You Learned

A strong student project is more than a working screen. It shows the problem, the decisions, the evidence, the limits, and what the team learned.

## A five-minute demo

1. **Problem (45 seconds):** Who struggles today, and why does it matter?
2. **User (30 seconds):** Introduce one realistic student scenario.
3. **Journey (2 minutes):** Profile → search → match → evidence → saved next step.
4. **Build choices (1 minute):** Explain one data, design, architecture, or AI decision.
5. **Evidence and next step (45 seconds):** Show testing evidence, limitations, and the next improvement.

Keep a backup recording or screenshots. A demo should not depend on a live service, private account, or unverified claim.

## What to include in the project record

- Problem statement and target audience.
- Before-and-after user journey.
- Architecture or data-flow diagram.
- Source and verification log for sample opportunities.
- Test cases, usability findings, and changes made because of them.
- Known limitations, risks, and out-of-scope features.
- Each teammate’s contribution and review evidence.

## Reflection prompts

Answer these individually, using specific examples:

- What did I make, test, explain, or improve?
- Which decision changed after evidence or feedback?
- What did I understand only after something failed?
- How did I use AI, and how did I verify its output?
- What would I do differently with one more week?
- What is one skill I can now teach another student?

## Honest claims

Say “our prototype suggests” rather than “we proved” when the sample is small. Distinguish a mockup from a deployed feature, sample data from verified production data, and a match explanation from an admission prediction. Credit sources, libraries, teammates, and AI assistance.

## Portfolio checklist

Before sharing publicly, remove secrets and private data, check that screenshots show no personal information, link to the repository and live demo, explain how to run it, include accessibility and privacy decisions, and state what is unfinished. The goal is to make the work understandable and reusable by the next student.
